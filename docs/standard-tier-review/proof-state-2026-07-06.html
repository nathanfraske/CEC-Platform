Owed, in order of impact

The gates that move capabilities into the right-hand column.

01Connector fit-check and thermal soak (OQ-86)

One sample order proves the blade seats in the receptacle and unlocks the whole output architecture. It also gives the decisive thermal datum. Specific unknowns: receptacle across-thickness depth (~3.7 mm drawn; over 4.0 mm forces a wider pitch), gang insertion force, detent engagement, and contact-resistance spread (bench acceptance at spread ratio ~0.21 to 0.28).

02Acquisition and alerting firmware

The 1 kHz ring buffer with threshold freeze, per-pin imbalance alert, energy integration, cross-module synchronized capture, persist-on-fault, HID sensors, standalone mode. Spec'd, unwritten. This is what turns the sensing hardware into the product.

03Bench accuracy measurement

Put a fab board on a bench against a reference meter and confirm the ~0.5% number that is today a calculation.

04Route and fab the rest of the beta line

EPS and both PCIe SKUs are placed with zero copper routed. The three daughterboards are routed and DRC-clean but not fabbed. The 24-pin rev3 and the C6-MCU refresh are in CAD.

05Thermal envelope and EMC posture

The daughterboard board-level worst case (F2) needs the soak plus a conduction-sink model or an owner envelope statement. The no-Wi-Fi, unintentional-radiator posture is a design decision, not a certified result.

The one-line summary: the concept is validated and two boards are manufacturing-ready; the engineering is proven in the design tools and against real manufacturer data; the bench measurements, the connector fit-check, and the firmware are the owed work.
