## Survey 8: Radio-free module MCU — recommendation for D-ENT-5

**Recommendation: pick (a), radio-free MCU — not (b).** Primary part family: **STM32G4** (STM32G431 for the I2C/digital-sensor families — 24-pin, EPS, PCIe; STM32G474 for the ADC-heavy 12VHPWR Standard). **ESP32-P4 is confirmed genuinely radio-free** and is a credible, recommended *alternative/complement* — it already is the ENT-AIR-compliant MCU for 12VHPWR Pro today, at zero extra cost, since Pro already specs ESP32-P4 platform-wide. But P4 is not the free lunch the hypothesis hoped for: it requires the **same order of hardware-redesign effort** as a non-Espressif migration (no SIP/MINI module exists — see (e)), it's oversized/overpriced for the lightweight digital modules, and it does not resolve a secondary trust axis (China-origin silicon) that some ENT-AIR buyers will raise independently of "does it have a radio." STM32G4 answers both axes at a lower BOM cost with a stronger ADC/comparator fit. Option (b) (fused-off/antenna-absent ESP32) is **credibility-weak** on the evidence found — see §(c) — and I would not carry it forward as the ratified posture for the strictest ENT-AIR buyer segment.

### Per-family fit notes

- **24-pin, EPS, PCIe (digital/I2C-sensor families):** these barely touch the module MCU's ADC at all — sensing rides I2C to the INA228/INA238 parts, §6.13 detection is a GPIO latch off an external comparator, DETECT-pin sensing happens on the **Hub's** ADC, not the module's. The MCU envelope here is genuinely light: CAN + I2C(2 pins) + a handful of GPIO for the §6.13 latches + USB for flashing. Every candidate in the table satisfies this trivially. The one concrete, already-documented internal signal that migration would *help* here: `module-requirements-24pin.md` REQ-24PIN-COMMON-003 flags that "**the C6 GPIO budget SHALL be verified to bound the monitored-rail count before layout**" on the rev3 24-pin — i.e., the current C6-MINI-1's pin budget is already tight against the §6.13 per-rail latch count. STM32G4 (35–50 GPIO on a 48/64-pin package) or CH32V307 (80 GPIO) directly relieves that constraint rather than just working around it.
- **12VHPWR Standard (raw-ADC family):** this is the board that actually stresses ADC channel count (6× per-pin current + 1 rail divider + 2 NTC + REF3030 monitor ≈ 10 analog inputs today, spec's stated floor is "7+"). STM32G474's 5×ADC/42-channel envelope and built-in fast comparators are the best match found — comfortably clears the requirement with room for the sideband taps, and could eventually let the §6.13 comparator move on-die. ESP32-P4's 12–16 channels also clears it, with the channel-count caveat below.
- **12VHPWR Pro:** already ESP32-P4 today, platform-wide, independent of ENT-AIR. **If D-ENT-5 ratifies "radio-free MCU," this module needs zero additional hardware work** — it's a live, already-existing proof that a radio-free MCU fits the platform's own higher-sensing-tier envelope (LTC2358-18, ~900 kB/s RS-485, CAN control all already spec'd onto P4).
- **Hub (Standard/Pro):** out of scope for this survey (module MCUs only), but worth flagging: Hub Pro is also already P4-based per the board table, reinforcing the same "already in-family" argument if the owner wants ENT-AIR's Hub-side story to lean on P4 while modules lean STM32G4.

### The fused-off/antenna-absent (b) credibility story — checked, and it's weak

1. **No antenna-absent SKU exists**, confirmed for both current chips: ESP32-S3-MINI-1 and ESP32-C6-MINI-1 each ship in exactly two variants — the PCB-antenna module (MINI-1) and an external-antenna-**connector** module (MINI-1U). MINI-1U is not a "radio disabled" part: the RF transceiver die is fully live either way; the "U" variant only relocates where the antenna element physically attaches. There is no Espressif SKU where the RF front-end silicon itself is absent or disconnected from the die.
2. **No comprehensive, vendor-documented Wi-Fi eFuse-disable exists on the chips actually in use.** I pulled Espressif's own `esp_efuse_table.csv` for ESP32-S3 directly (primary source, not a search snippet) — it has zero fields named for WIFI/BT/BLE/RADIO disable; its `DIS_*` fields only cover cache, download-mode, JTAG, USB, and CAN. Community forum threads on the *original* ESP32 confirm a `DISABLE_BT` eFuse exists there but explicitly note **no equivalent Wi-Fi-disable eFuse exists even on that chip**; nothing found suggests S3/C6 close that gap. This means "fuse it off" is not currently a vendor-supported, verifiable primitive for Wi-Fi on our silicon — any implementation would be a firmware-enforced "never call the radio init API" posture layered on secure boot + JTAG-lock, not a silicon-level hardware disable.
3. **The applicable security-standard bar structurally disfavors this.** DoD/Army wireless-security guidance (Army Pam 25-2-9) defines "physically disabled" as disablement that **cannot** be effected "through logical settings configured by software... or otherwise configured in volatile or non-volatile memory or storage" — an eFuse-gated radio sits in a real gray zone against that language (it's an irreversible physical action, but the gating mechanism is itself a non-volatile-memory bit). The concrete remedy SCIF/DoD guidance names instead is "**antenna, radio module... removed or physically disabled**" — i.e., physical removal of a separable part. That's structurally impossible on a monolithic ESP32 SoC, where the RF baseband shares silicon with the CPU/CAN/ADC the module needs to keep.
4. **Reputational headwind, not directly applicable but relevant to buyer trust:** the March 2025 Tarlogic finding (29 undocumented Bluetooth HCI commands, CVE-2025-27840, widely reported as "hidden commands in 1 billion Bluetooth chips") affects the *original* ESP32 only — Espressif's own advisory states it does **not** affect the ESP32-C/S/H series our modules use. I'm not claiming our chips are vulnerable. But the episode is exactly the kind of precedent that makes "trust our fuse" a harder sell to a buyer already primed to distrust undocumented behavior in this vendor's RF stack — it's a brand-level credibility tax, not a technical one.
5. **No precedent found either way** of a security-conscious buyer segment formally accepting "present-but-fused" radio silicon as equivalent to radio-absent hardware. Everything found (SCIF device policy, NIST AC-18(3)) points toward physical absence as the accepted bar, not fusing.

Net: (b)'s "IF the target-buyer evidence shows it is acceptable" condition in REQ-MOD-AIR-020 is **not met** by anything found here for the strictest (SCIF/DoD-adjacent) reading of "ENT-AIR." It might still be workable for a buyer whose air-gap requirement is closer to "no radio protocols are ever used" than to hardened anti-implant assurance — but that's a target-buyer-profile question the plan's customer-intake work should answer before leaning on (b), not something this survey can ratify on its own.

### Inspection/verification story (mirrors REQ-HUB-AIR-101 / feeds REQ-MOD-AIR-021)

- **Option (a):** trivially satisfies "visually verifiable at inspection without powering the unit." A different MCU part number/package is laser-marked on the board and printed on the BOM/CPL — an STM32G474RET6 in LQFP-64 next to zero PCB-antenna keepout trace is unmistakably not the consumer board at a glance, no power or tooling required. This is a strictly stronger, cheaper verification story than anything (b) can offer.
- **Option (b):** fails the same bar on inspection — a fused unit is visually **identical** to a stock consumer board (same MINI-1 module, same silkscreen, same antenna keepout region physically present even if traced-out/unpopulated). Confirming the fuse state requires an active eFuse-read session (powered, via download mode) or trusting a printed serial number against a manufacturing record — a paperwork/trust story, not a physical-inspection one, and it arguably violates the "without powering the unit" clause outright.

### Risks

- **ESP32-P4 ADC channel count is unverified precisely** — sources disagree (12 vs 14 vs 16 channels across 2 SAR units). Comfortably clears the 7+ requirement either way, but confirm against the chip TRM's electrical-characteristics table before committing hardware.
- **Firmware NRE is real and currently unscoped.** Every non-Espressif candidate is a full port (new HAL, CAN driver, ADC driver, and — most consequentially — re-establishing an equivalent secure-boot/firmware-signing story to match REQ-MOD-COMMON-012's parity-with-Hub requirement on a new vendor's silicon). This survey doesn't estimate that cost; it should be scoped by firmware engineering before D-ENT-5 locks a part.
- **Origin-trust is a second, separate axis from radio.** GD32F303 and CH32V307 fix the radio problem at the best price/GPIO ratio in this survey, but remain China-origin silicon (GigaDevice/Beijing, WCH/Nanjing) — they do not close a stricter buyer's supply-chain-origin concern. Note Espressif itself is also a Chinese company, so this is not a *new* exposure introduced by staying on ESP32 either — but if the owner wants one part that resolves both the radio question and the origin question, only STM32 (ST/Switzerland-listed, Franco-Italian), NXP (Netherlands), or TI (USA) do that; P4/GD32/CH32V do not.
- **Dual-MCU-BOM is an ongoing tax, not a one-time cost.** Every future spec change (a new sensing feature, a §6.x revision) has to be designed, verified, and maintained twice — once per MCU family — for as long as ENT-AIR modules exist as a hardware-divergent variant. This is a real, recurring engineering-process cost the owner should weigh, though it is *consistent* with the platform's already-ratified pattern of enterprise-tier hardware diverging from consumer where warranted (PolarFire vs the consumer Hub compute, D-ENT-2).
- **Fleet-wide migration (replacing ESP32 everywhere, not just ENT-AIR) is not recommended** — it would discard a large amount of already-routed/fab-direction board work (Hub Standard, 12VHPWR Standard, EPS, PCIe, 24-pin) for a benefit (radio-free posture) that only the ENT-AIR segment needs; consumer/Pro tiers get no upside and lose the ESP-IDF ecosystem/toolchain investment along with the spec's own stated forward-looking Wi-Fi option on Hub Standard.
- **Supply-chain snapshot volatility observed directly in this research:** both the current baseline part (ESP32-S3-MINI-1-N4R2) and the recommended alternative (NXP S32K118) showed as out-of-stock at their respective distributors at pull time (S32K118 specifically flagged a 30-week lead time) — treat all stock figures here as a point-in-time snapshot, not a sourcing guarantee, and re-check before BOM lock.
- **No official Espressif SIP/MINI module exists for P4 yet** (confirmed via an ESP32 forum thread asking exactly this in 2025, unanswered as of this pull) — third-party core boards exist (Waveshare, M5Stack, Olimex) but the platform would be an early adopter of a from-scratch P4 layout, with less community-proven reference-design depth than the mature MINI-1/C6-MINI-1 footprints enjoy today.
