## Survey 10: 100BASE-T1 module link — recommendation set

**Context grounded against:** REQ-MOD-COMMON-003, REQ-HUB-COMMON-043/106, OQ-80 (`docs/owner-queue.md`), `module-ent-spec-sheets.md` §0/§2, `hub-ent-variants-plan.md` §3, survey-1 (PolarFire sizing: MPFS095TS = 93K LE / 4 SERDES / 2 hardened GEMs), survey-2 (uplink PHY precedent), survey-8 (radio-free MCU: STM32G4 primary, ESP32-P4 confirmed radio-free), and the pre-3rd-ruling BOM-C detailed pass (`bom-c-module-if-base-secio.md`, which already priced an 8×THVD1450DR RS-485 receiver bank at $4.93/hub — now the "keep compat" baseline this survey prices against).

1. **Module-side T1 PHY: TI DP83TC814S-Q1** (real stock, no NDA friction found) as the safe default; **NXP TJA1103** ($1.49@100q, cheapest confirmed + has 1588) as the cost/feature leader *if* its NDA-for-docs-and-software posture (confirmed directly off NXP's own product page) is cleared with NXP first — flagged, not silently resolved.
2. **Hub-side 8-port architecture: 2× Microchip LAN9370** (4-port 100BASE-T1 switch each, integrated PHYs + 802.1AS/1588v2 hardware timestamping on-chip) bridged into the PolarFire fabric via 2 lightweight RGMII/MII MAC instances — a better-fitting answer than any of the three options the task named (soft-everything-in-fabric / SJA1105 / SJA1110 / KSZ9897-aggregation), found during this survey and cross-checked by the dedicated research agent against the literal options asked about.
3. **Module MCU: ESP32-P4**, uniformly across all RMII-needing ENT streaming families (EPS, PCIe-2/3, 12VHPWR) — reusing the already-proven 12VHPWR Pro hardware design as the internal P4 reference, rather than splitting the ENT module lineup across a third toolchain (STM32H5) for EPS/PCIe alone. STM32H563-class is documented below as a fully viable, price-competitive fallback.
4. **Dual-mode verdict: drop RS-485 backward compat on ENT hub ports (T1-only).** No vendor precedent exists for bridging a live T1 PHY MDI to an RS-485 transceiver on the same pair (checked directly against TI/NXP datasheets by both this survey and the dedicated research agent), the platform already has an exact-fit precedent for the alternative (§8 graceful degrade — a Pro module's RS-485 already goes dark on a Standard Hub today), and dropping compat avoids $8.93–29.93/hub in mux + receiver-bank cost.
5. **Sync accuracy: sub-µs is a realistic, industry-standard target for hardware-timestamped gPTP over 100BASE-T1** (IEEE 802.1AS's own design target is <1µs end-to-end over ≤7 hops; CEC's topology is ~2 hops), achievable with the recommended silicon (ESP32-P4's confirmed HW timestamp+PPS, LAN9370's confirmed 802.1AS/1588v2 HW timestamping) — but no vendor publishes a single "X ns for this exact topology" number, so this needs bench verification before being load-bearing, and REQ-HUB-COMMON-106's own SYNC-vs-LATENCY distinction is independently confirmed correct (6.72µs frame time at 100 Mbps, verified twice by direct arithmetic).

### 4. Dual-mode port cost — T1 + RS-485 sharing pins 4/5

**No vendor precedent found, in either direction (permitting or forbidding), for bridging a T1 PHY's MDI to a foreign transceiver** — checked directly against TI and NXP datasheets by both this survey and the dedicated research agent. The derivable electrical risk is real: TJA1100's own datasheet confirms the PHY keeps an **active ~100Ω-class termination/wake-detect network engaged on the MDI pair in every standby/sleep state short of removing VDD entirely** (it must, to hear a remote wake-up pulse) — so a "firmware-idled" T1 PHY left bridged onto a live RS-485 link is a genuine loading/signal-integrity hazard no datasheet gives confidence on without bench validation. THVD1450's receiver is comparatively light (~96 kΩ, derived from its 1/8-unit-load spec) but its pin capacitance is unquantified — and 100BASE-T1's own ESD-diode vendors sell sub-3pF (and sub-1pF) parts specifically because the link is that sensitive to parasitics.

**Recommendation: drop RS-485 compat.** Four independent reasons converge: (1) no vendor precedent for pair-sharing exists either way — building a safety/SI-sensitive shared node on an undocumented interaction is a real risk for a feature that isn't load-bearing; (2) the platform already has an exact-fit precedent for the alternative — REQ-HUB-COMMON-045's own "never fail to function" language is satisfied because a legacy Pro module's CAN control+telemetry stays fully live on an ENT hub, with only its streaming pair going dark, **identical to how a Pro module's RS-485 already goes dark on a Standard Hub today** (§8 graceful degrade, an already-shipped, already-understood pattern); (3) dropping compat is a net cost *saving* against the already-drafted BOM-C ($4.93 removed, not just a new $0 avoided); (4) a mux control line that must be correctly sequenced with T1 PHY power state is itself a new way to accidentally bridge two live drivers — added failure surface for a compatibility feature, not a safety feature.

### 5. Sync accuracy statement

- **Frame time at 100 Mbps, independently verified twice** (this survey + the research agent, same arithmetic): preamble+SFD (8B) + minimum frame (64B) + IFG (96 bit / 12B) = 84B = 672 bits ÷ 100 Mbps = **6.72 µs**. Confirms REQ-HUB-COMMON-106's own "~7 µs" figure.
- **Sync accuracy is not frame latency.** PTP's four-timestamp exchange (t1..t4) computes `offset = ((t2−t1) − (t4−t3))/2` — it explicitly solves for and cancels the link's transit/queuing delay, so residual clock-alignment error depends on timestamp *precision and symmetry*, not on how long a frame takes to cross the wire. [Meinberg: "The accuracy... is not affected by the absolute propagation delay of the network packets... as long as the delay is constant"]
- **IEEE 802.1AS's own design target: <1µs end-to-end synchronization accuracy for ≤7 hops, steady state** (802.1AS-REV extends toward ≤64 hops at 1µs). CEC's module-to-hub topology is effectively **1–2 hops** (module MCU → T1 PHY/LAN9370 boundary clock → fabric), well inside that envelope with large margin.
- **Practical accuracy ladder found** (Promwad, automotive/embedded engineering source, internally consistent with the 802.1AS target above): hardware-timestamped gPTP with transparent switches ≈ 50–200 ns; MAC-level hardware timestamping alone ≈ 200 ns–1 µs; software timestamping ≈ 10–100 µs; **AUTOSAR time-sync-over-CAN ≈ 50–500 µs** (context: this is why the platform's *existing* CAN control channel could never have hit this target — T1+PTP is a genuine, not incremental, capability step).
- **Confirmed hardware timestamp support exists at every layer of the recommended stack**: ESP32-P4 (MAC-level, with PPS output), LAN9370/9374 (on-chip, 802.1AS/1588v2), and PolarFire SoC's own hardened GEMs (built-in Timestamping Unit, confirmed via Microchip's own PolarFire SoC documentation and mainline Linux PTP support landing v2025.10.1) — plus Microchip's own **TimeProvider 4500**, a PolarFire-SoC-based PTP grandmaster product marketed at sub-nanosecond accuracy, exists as an existence proof this silicon family is credible at the high end (explicitly *not* cited as a number to design against — it's a specialized appliance, not a stand-in for this Hub).
- **Honest gap:** no vendor (Microchip in particular, for Core1588/CoreTSE) publishes a single "±X ns for this exact topology" figure. The sub-µs claim is well-triangulated across IEEE 802.1AS's own target + a converging industry accuracy ladder + confirmed hardware timestamp primitives at every stage — but it is a *design target achievable with this class of hardware*, not a datasheet-guaranteed number, and needs bench measurement before REQ-HUB-COMMON-106 is treated as verified rather than targeted.

### Electrical coexistence (pins 4/5, shared FTP Cat5e-class cable)

- **100BASE-T1 signaling confirmed:** PAM3 modulation, 66.66 MBd single-pair full-duplex via echo cancellation (4B3B/3B2T line coding, IEEE 802.3bw/802.3bp). Spectral content is baseband, concentrated below ~33 MHz.
- **No isolation transformer typically needed.** Standard automotive/industrial T1 front-end practice is a **common-mode choke (CMC) + AC-coupling capacitors**, not a transformer — isolation transformers exist for T1 (Halo, Pulse) but are only pulled in when galvanic isolation is specifically required, which CEC's within-one-chassis, common-ground topology does not obviously need (same reasoning as in-vehicle T1 links). Real, priced CMC part: **TDK ACT1210L-201-2P-TL00**, OPEN Alliance 100BASE-T1-compliant, 1210 case, **$0.30–1.56** depending on tier ([LCSC](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C131444.html) / [DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/tdk-corporation/ACT1210L-201-2P-TL00/6817006)); Pulse AE0100/AE1210/AE2002 family is the direct alternative.
- **100BASE-T1 typically runs over *unshielded* twisted pair** — CEC's platform-locked FTP (shielded) Cat5e-class cable is a strict superset of what T1 itself requires, adding margin, not creating a new requirement.
- **T568B pair mapping (verified against the locked pin table):** pins 4/5 (the T1 pair) are **T568B's actual Pair 1** — the tightest, most centrally-twisted pair in the connector, adjacent in the shell to pins 3/6 (CAN, T568B's split Pair 3 — CLAUDE.md's own description of pins 3/6 as "the T568B split pair" is confirmed correct). This means the primary near-field coupling risk sits right at the untwisted region of the RJ-45 termination (pin 4 physically neighbors pin 3, pin 5 neighbors pin 6), not along the cable run itself, where each pair's own twist dominates.
- **Cat5e pair-to-pair NEXT is well-characterized and favorable at the relevant frequencies:** 45.8 dB @ 20 MHz, 35.3 dB @ 100 MHz (isolation *improves* at lower frequency) — meaning CAN's low-frequency content sits in the *better*-isolated part of the band, and T1's baseband content (mostly <33 MHz) sits comfortably inside the same favorable region.
- **No direct precedent exists for "T1 sharing one 8P8C connector/cable with classical CAN"** — automotive practice tests T1 EMC/crosstalk against *bundled separate cables* (OPEN Alliance TC1/TC3 CMC spec, 4-around-1 harness bundling), not multiple signal types sharing one modular connector the way CEC's design does. The underlying physics (twisted-pair NEXT performance, CAN's low frequency content, T1's own EMC-hardened line coding) supports feasibility, but this is a genuinely CEC-specific integration with no off-the-shelf validation — **recommend a bench SI/EMC verification pass before shipping**, consistent with the repo's own existing practice for exactly this class of question (the §3.1 CAN 1 Mbps star/stub SI bench test).
- **ESD protection, real parts found:** **Nexperia PESD2ETH100-T** — explicitly "fully OPEN Alliance 100BASE-T1 compliant," <3pF, SOT23, AEC-Q101, **$0.21@100q** ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/nexperia-usa-inc/PESD2ETH100-TR/11205897), 8,689 in stock). **onsemi ESD7205** has a better capacitance spec (0.4pF @ 1MHz) at a similar price ($0.28@100q) but the exact checked package is currently out of stock at DigiKey — Nexperia is the stronger pick on pure sourcing grounds today.

### Draft REQ refinement text

**REQ-MOD-COMMON-003 (grounds the MCU pick, per its own "survey 10 grounds" language):**

> Enterprise streaming modules SHALL carry 100BASE-T1 single-pair Ethernet on pair 2 (pins 4/5) — bidirectional, replacing the Pro-tier RS-485 for the ENT line — with DETECT = the locked 10 kΩ CAN+100BASE-T1 class, and SHALL participate in the fleet sub-µs time synchronization (REQ-HUB-COMMON-106). MCU: **ESP32-P4** (RMII-only per ESP-IDF, confirmed hardware IEEE 1588/PTP timestamping with PPS output, confirmed radio-free) is RECOMMENDED uniformly across all RMII-needing families (EPS, PCIe-2/3, 12VHPWR), extending the already-committed 12VHPWR Pro/ENT MCU rather than introducing a third module toolchain; STM32H563-class (confirmed RMII + 1588-2008/PTPv2, confirmed radio-free, ~$4.29–4.74@100q) is a documented, price-competitive fallback if vendor consolidation with the STM32G4 (24-pin) firmware investment is weighted more heavily. STM32G4 remains confirmed unable to serve any streaming family — no MAC-integrated 100BASE-T1 part exists to rescue it (checked: the SPI-MACPHY convenience trend has reached 10BASE-T1S only, not 100BASE-T1). Non-streaming enterprise builds (24-pin) stay CAN-only on STM32G431, pair 2 terminated per the locked table. [PROPOSED per survey 10 — owner review]

**REQ-HUB-COMMON-043 (resolves its own "sub-choice, survey 10" gate):**

> The Hub SHALL service the module streaming pair (pins 4/5) on all 8 ports as **100BASE-T1 only** (ENT modules, DETECT 10 kΩ). RS-485 backward compatibility for Pro-tier consumer modules is DROPPED on the enterprise Hub — a Pro module's streaming pair goes dark on an ENT port, identical to the existing §8 pattern of a Pro module's RS-485 going dark on a Standard Hub; CAN control and telemetry remain fully serviced (REQ-HUB-COMMON-045 unaffected). Rationale (survey 10): no vendor precedent exists for bridging a live T1 PHY MDI to an RS-485 transceiver on the shared pair, and the mux-based alternative costs $8.93–29.93/hub against $0 for the drop. Hub-side T1 termination: 2× Microchip LAN9370 (4-port 100BASE-T1 switch, integrated 802.1AS/1588v2 hardware timestamping) bridged into the PolarFire fabric via 2 RGMII/MII MAC instances, superseding the originally-scoped 8-discrete-PHY/soft-switch/8×THVD1450DR receiver-bank designs. [PROPOSED per survey 10 — owner review; strikes the "unless the owner explicitly drops... sub-choice" clause by exercising it]

**REQ-HUB-COMMON-106:** no change proposed — this survey's independent frame-time arithmetic (6.72µs) and accuracy-ladder research (IEEE 802.1AS <1µs/≤7-hops target, converging industry figures) both support the requirement as drafted. Add a grounding citation to this survey when it lands.
