# Explaining CEC to someone who bought a PC and never opens it

This is the third audience, below the enthusiast. Someone who bought a prebuilt PC at a
big-box store or online, uses it, and has no idea what's inside it. They don't swap parts.
They don't open the case. They don't read tech forums. A diagnosis is useless to them
because they can't act on it. So the whole framing from the enthusiast sheet
(`standard-plain-language.md`) has to change. Companion visual: `standard-bundle-everyone.html`.

## What this person cares about

- The computer costing them a lot of money and lasting.
- Not losing what's on it (photos, schoolwork, their kid's games).
- Not being scared of it, and not having to learn how it works.
- Not getting brushed off by support, and not being overcharged by a shop.
- Not having it become a fire risk under their desk.

They do not care about telemetry, diagnosis, rails, or connectors. Don't mention any of it.

## The benefit, in their terms

CEC watches the parts of their computer that can fail, and tells them in plain words whether
it's okay. A light on the box: green means healthy. If something's wrong, it says so and says
what to do. That's the whole product to this person. No numbers, no app, no learning.

Three things make it worth having for someone who can't fix anything themselves:

1. **Peace of mind you can see.** A glance at the light tells them their expensive computer is
   healthy right now. That's a real, daily reassurance, the same reason people like a light on
   a smoke alarm or a home camera.
2. **A warning before danger.** Some computers have a high-power cable that can overheat and,
   in the worst case, melt or catch fire. This person has no way to know that's happening. CEC
   warns them early, in plain words: unplug it and get it looked at. For someone who can't see
   it coming any other way, that warning is the most valuable thing it does.
3. **Proof when they need help.** When the computer acts up, support often says "we can't find
   anything" and a shop often guesses and charges. CEC hands whoever helps them the exact
   reason. That turns a brush-off into an honored warranty, and a guessing repair into a fast,
   cheap, correct one.

## The honest part about how it gets there

This person will never install clip-on sensors inside a case. So for them, CEC arrives one of
two ways: it comes already inside the computer they bought, or a shop installs it once. After
that they do nothing. It watches on its own, and the only interface they ever touch is the
light and a plain notification.

**This is a real strategic point, not just copy.** Serving this buyer means the channel is the
company that builds the prebuilt, or the shop that services it, not the buyer directly. The
pitch to the buyer is peace of mind and protection. The pitch to the builder or shop is a
warranty-cost and support-cost reducer, and a safety feature they can advertise.

## The tiers of copy

**One sentence:**
> CEC is a little guardian for your computer that tells you, in plain words, if it's healthy,
> and warns you before an expensive part fails or becomes a danger.

**The short version:**
> You don't need to know anything about computers. CEC watches the parts inside yours that can
> fail, shows a green light when everything's fine, and tells you plainly if something's wrong
> and what to do. If you ever need it repaired or replaced under warranty, it hands the shop
> the exact reason, so you're not brushed off and not overcharged.

**Why you (pick three):**
- **See that it's okay, at a glance.** A light, not a dashboard. Green means healthy.
- **Get warned before danger.** If the power inside starts to overheat, you hear about it
  early, in plain words, while it's still safe to act.
- **Never get brushed off.** If it does go wrong, you have proof of what, so warranty and
  repair go your way.
- **Nothing to learn, nothing to do.** It's installed once and watches on its own. You just
  keep using your computer.

**The reassuring close:**
> You'll never have to open your computer, read a chart, or learn a thing. CEC does the
> watching. You keep using your PC.

## What not to say to this person

- No "power supply," "graphics card connector," "rails," "voltage," "current," "telemetry."
  Say "the parts inside that can fail" and "the power."
- No diagnosis story. They can't act on a diagnosis. The value is the warning and the proof,
  not the data.
- No install-it-yourself. Assume it's already in the machine or a shop puts it in.
- No fear-mongering. State the fire risk plainly and once, paired with the reassurance that
  this is the thing that watches for it.
