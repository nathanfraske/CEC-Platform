# OQ-10: High-Current Vertical Transition Methods for PCB Shunt Sites (CEC EPS/PCIe Current-Sense)

## TL;DR
- **For 40–55 A EPS/PCIe shunt sites on 2–4-layer FR-4 at JLCPCB-class fabs, the recommended default is to AVOID the vertical transition at the shunt itself: keep the high-current path on the outer layer through the shunt on a 2 oz copper pour/fat trace, and place any required layer-change via field OUTSIDE the Kelvin sense window** — because the shunt (0.5 mΩ) is the dominant resistance by design, and a via field's ~37 µΩ corrupts the measurement only if it sits inside the sense loop.
- **Where a transition is unavoidable, a paralleled via field (the default) is sufficient and cheapest**: ~22 vias carry 55 A by raw division at the corpus operating point, but published practice adds margin for non-uniform sharing (≥30 symmetric vias); copper coins and plated slots buy real conductivity but solve a thermal/space problem CEC does not have, and copper coin is not a self-serve JLCPCB option.
- **Several numbers must be confirmed at the JLCPCB quote tool**: guaranteed minimum barrel plating (no published µm spec; ~18 µm relayed via support), 2 oz price adder, 4-layer POFV charge, copper-coin availability/price, and plated-slot minimums.

## Key Findings
1. The measurement-accuracy question is dominated by **placement relative to the Kelvin sense points**, not by which transition method is chosen. A 22-via field has ~37 µΩ resistance — about 7% of the 500 µΩ shunt — which is fully excluded from the measurement if it lies outside the four-wire sense window, and fully corrupting if it lies inside it.
2. A standard 0.3 mm / 1 oz via carries roughly **2.4–2.5 A at a 10 °C rise**, so 55 A needs ~22 vias by raw division; published rule-of-thumb practice keeps each via to 0.5–1 A and recommends a 50% margin for current crowding, pushing the count to ~30+.
3. **Copper coin** is a thermal-management/high-density technology; for a pure conduction problem where the shunt is the dominant resistance, it buys nothing a sufficient via field or a fat outer-layer pour does not — and it is not orderable self-serve at JLCPCB.
4. **Plated slots** give more barrel perimeter per unit area than round vias (a 2 × 0.5 mm slot ≈ 6 round 0.3 mm vias) but introduce solder-wicking risk near SMD pads and are unnecessary if the transition is moved off-pad.
5. **Filled-and-capped via-in-pad (IPC-4761 Type VII)** uses non-conductive resin in the standard process — the fill carries no current — and at JLCPCB it is free only on 6–20 layer boards, charged on 4-layer, and effectively unavailable on 2-layer. Putting vias directly under the shunt terminals is discouraged for Kelvin-sensed low-value shunts.
6. **The top-layer-only alternative** requires a very wide conductor (≈20 mm at 2 oz, ≈39 mm at 1 oz for 55 A at 30 °C rise external), which is large but eliminates the transition's reliability and accuracy risks entirely and is well-supported by polygon-pour practice.

## Details

### 0. Design context: what 40–55 A at 12 V means here
The EPS/PCIe 8-pin power connector uses the Molex Mini-Fit Jr. contact system, rated up to 8 A per circuit when populated with three current circuits (Molex spec PS-45558-001-001). With three 12 V circuits that gives 3 × 8 A × 12 V = 288 W per connector — i.e., ~24 A at 12 V per 8-pin connector at the connector's rated derating; the often-quoted PCIe "150 W" figure is a conservative graphics-card power-budget convention, not the connector's contact limit (Molex datasheet breakdown via GPUMiningResources; Exxact Corp., "PCIe 8-pin vs EPS-12V 8-pin power connections"; smps.us, "atx12vo connectors," citing the Intel/ATX design-guide 8 A/pin figure). The 40–55 A per-shunt design target therefore corresponds to a high-power rail or an aggregated/12VHPWR-class path spanning more than one connector's worth of current.

The sense element is the Bourns **CSS2H-2512R-L500x: 0.5 mΩ, 6 W power rating at 70 °C terminal temperature**, a Cu-Mn EB-welded metal strip, **±100 ppm/°C including the copper terminals**, operating range −55 to +170 °C, in a 2512 footprint with separate sense-terminal pads in the recommended layout (Bourns CSS2H-2512 datasheet, Rev. 01/26: Electrical Characteristics table and Recommended Pad Layout). At 55 A the shunt dissipates I²R = 55² × 0.0005 ≈ **1.5 W**, comfortably inside its 6 W rating.

### 1. Via field engineering for 40–55 A — the quantitative basis

**Per-via capacity.** IPC-2152 (2009), *Standard for Determining Current-Carrying Capacity in Printed Board Design*, is the load-bearing standard but, as multiple sources note, does not give an explicit via-current formula; the IPC-2221 area formula I = k·ΔT^0.44·A^0.725 (k = 0.048 external, 0.024 internal) is applied to the barrel's annular copper cross-section (smps.us IPC-2152 calculator page; PCBSync, "IPC-2152 Explained"). Fabricator/tool implementations converge on: a 0.3 mm finished-hole via with ~25.4 µm (1 oz/1 mil) plating has a copper cross-section of about 7.5 sq mil — "roughly equivalent to a 7.5 mil wide trace" — supporting ≈2.4 A at a 10 °C rise (semiconductor.tools, "PCB Via Current Capacity"; getzenquery PCB Via Current Calculator: "0.3 mm via / 25 µm plating → ≈ 2.4 A"); the same geometry at the thinner 20 µm budget-fab plating drops to ≈0.8–1.0 A at 10 °C rise (queenems, "PCB Via Current Capacity"). For reference, Sierra Circuits' IPC-2221 via calculator returns 3.07 A for a 10-mil via, 1 oz, at a 40 °C rise. The corpus operating point — 2.5 A/via, ~0.033 mm² barrel cross-section, ~75 A/mm² current density — is internally consistent (2.5 A ÷ 0.033 mm² = 75.8 A/mm²) and matches the getzenquery note that vias tolerate 60–100 A/mm² but flagging above 70 A/mm².

**Via count for 55 A.** Raw division: 55 A ÷ 2.5 A = **22 vias**. But current does not share equally. Peer-reviewed/conference work (arXiv 1801.08281, "Modeling and Simulation of Electromigration Behavior for Via Array Structure"; arXiv 1712.08722, "Uneven Current Distribution Modeling…") shows via-array current splits track path resistance: when feed-wire currents differ, the split becomes non-uniform, and FEM/SPICE resistive-mesh models reproduce this within ~1%. The physical cause is current crowding — high current density in the region closest to the current-injection point (USPTO 6,897,563, "Current crowding reduction technique"; Wikipedia, "Current crowding"). The widely-used working rule (TraceWidthCalculator IPC-2221 via calculator) is to "use enough vias so each carries no more than 0.5–1 A to maintain low temperature rise"; queenems advises a 50% safety margin because "the first via it encounters will carry significantly more current than the fifth via in the array," and getzenquery recommends "adding 15% extra vias." Applying a 50% margin to the 22-via baseline gives **~33 vias**; at 15%, ~26. **Recommend ≥30 vias arranged in a symmetric field (e.g., 5×6 or 6×6).**

**Spacing/pattern.** Symmetry is the governing rule: a symmetrical layout is recommended (queenems), and because current concentrates at the field edge nearest the approach, vias should be distributed evenly across the full current-approach front rather than clustered. For currents above ~20 A, queenems recommends larger drills (0.5 mm+) over dozens of 0.2 mm vias because "larger holes plate much more uniformly." Plane resistance helps balance the split by forcing current to spread before entering the field (arXiv 1712.08722 resistive-mesh result).

**Thermal coupling.** Vias in a field heat each other; a via is a copper cylinder surrounded by FR-4, a poor thermal conductor (~0.3 W/m·K vs copper ~400 W/m·K; JLCPCB "Heavy Copper PCBs" blog), so an isolated-via rating overstates per-via capacity inside a dense field. semiconductor.tools states vias "retain heat and have a 20–30% lower current rating than a surface trace with the same cross-sectional area." This reinforces the margin above. (Published *quantitative* treatments of field-internal thermal coupling vs isolated-via ratings are thin; this 20–30% derate is tier-4 corroboration and should be treated as indicative, not load-bearing.)

**Resistance contribution and the measurement question.** Per-via barrel resistance, using ρ_Cu = 1.72 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m (annealed copper, bulk ~1.67–1.72 × 10⁻⁸; NIST, "Electrical resistivity of copper, gold, palladium, and silver"), a 1.6 mm board thickness, and a 0.3 mm drill / 25 µm wall (annulus ≈ 2.2–3.3 × 10⁻⁸ m²):
R_via = ρL/A = (1.72e-8 × 1.6e-3) / (2.2e-8 to 3.3e-8) ≈ **0.8 mΩ per via**.
22 in parallel → **≈ 37 µΩ**, which is **~7.5% of the 500 µΩ shunt**. This confirms the corpus math. **The decisive point: this 37 µΩ matters only if the via field sits inside the Kelvin sense loop.** Kelvin/four-wire sensing exists precisely to exclude series trace, solder, and via resistance from the measurement (Bourns App Note N1702, "Using Current Sense Resistors for Accurate Current Measurement"; Analog Devices / Analog Dialogue Vol. 46 No. 2, Marcus O'Sullivan, "Optimize High-Current Sensing Accuracy by Improving Pad Layout of Low-Value Shunt Resistors," which measured: "the 22% error associated with the solder resistance without using Kelvin sensing… an equivalent solder resistance of about 0.144 mΩ," and an optimum split-pad footprint with "expected measurement error of less than 1%," using a 0.5 mΩ ULRG3-2512-0M50 test part). If the via field is in the force/current path outside the two sense taps, its resistance is invisible to the ADC; if it is between the sense taps, it adds directly to the apparent shunt value (a ~7% reading error). **Placement, not method, dominates accuracy.**

### 2. Copper coin technology

**What it is.** A solid copper slug embedded in the board, in embedded (laminated, metallurgically bonded), press-fit (mechanical), or inlay/through ("Cu coin through") and buried/half-buried ("Cu coin half buried") variants; "H/I/T" denote coin shape (Saturn Electronics, "Copper Coin PCB"; PCBWay help-center, "What is Embedded copper PCB"; Highleap, "Copper Coin Structure Types"). It is primarily a **thermal** solution: PCBWay states copper-coin heat dissipation is "approximately twice as effective" as a via array and copper's thermal conductivity "exceeds that of any conductive dielectric prepreg by 30 to 200 times."

**Reliability.** The dominant concern is CTE mismatch: copper ~16–18 ppm/°C vs FR-4 ~14–17 ppm/°C in-plane but **50–70 ppm/°C through-thickness** (Highleap, "Copper Coin PCB Design"; SYS Technology CTE guide), creating cyclic shear stress at the copper–resin interface that can initiate delamination. Mitigations: a 0.1–0.2 mm relief/resin window around the coin perimeter, and qualification per IPC-6012 Class 3 and IPC-9701 with ≥1000 thermal cycles and >1.0 N/mm peel strength (Highleap, "Copper Coin PCB Reliability Test"). OFC copper reportedly survives 2,000–3,000 cycles (−40 to +150 °C) vs 1,500–2,000 for ETP (Highleap, "Copper Coin Material" — tier-3/4 vendor data).

**Availability/cost.** Copper coin is a **premium-fab capability** (Saturn Electronics, PCBWay, FX PCB, Highleap, Andwin all offer it as a specialty line). At JLCPCB it is **not a self-serve standard option**: JLCPCB published a "Copper Coin PCB" blog describing embedded/buried variants but gives no price tier and advises consulting the manufacturer early — i.e., custom-quote only (JLCPCB blog "Copper Coin PCB"; requires sales confirmation). Do not confuse with JLCPCB's standard copper-core MCPCB ("Direct Heatsink Copper-Cored PCB"), a different product.

**Verdict for CEC.** For a 55 A / 12 V **current-conduction** shunt site (not a heat-sinking problem), the shunt's 500 µΩ is the dominant and intentional resistance; a coin's lower spreading resistance is irrelevant to the measurement and unnecessary for conduction that a via field or fat pour already handles. **Copper coin is not recommended** here: it adds CTE-driven reliability risk, cost, and a non-self-serve fab dependency for no functional gain.

### 3. Plated slots for current

**Capability.** Minimum plated-slot width is **0.5 mm at PCBWay and PCBGOGO**, with a ≥2:1 length-to-width rule (PCBWay help-center, "Slots standard size"; PCBGOGO, "Plated-through slots"; PCBSync, "PCB Slot"). At JLCPCB the minimum metallized-slot drill is **0.65 mm** and minimum non-metallized routing bit **1.0 mm**, with round drills 0.15–6.30 mm in 0.05 mm steps (JLCPCB blog, "Guide to PCB Via Design"). Slots are NC-milled then plated identically to PTHs; high-current vendors offer 18–50 µm plating and aspect ratios to 4:1 (length:width, to avoid plating-solution trapping) or 6:1 at specialty houses (FanyPCB, "PCB Plated Through Slots," which claims "50 A+ in some designs" — tier-2/4 vendor claim).

**Perimeter/equivalence.** Plated cross-section ∝ perimeter × plating thickness. A 2 × 0.5 mm slot has plated perimeter ≈ 2L + πW = 2(2) + π(0.5) = **5.57 mm**, vs a round 0.3 mm via at πD = 0.94 mm. Ratio ≈ **5.9** — so one 2 × 0.5 mm slot replaces about **6 round 0.3 mm vias** (≈15 A at 2.5 A/via-equivalent). Carrying 55 A would take ~4 such slots.

**Assembly.** Slots at/near SMD shunt pads share the via-in-pad solder-wicking failure mode: open slots wick solder away from the joint (JLCPCB, "Free Via-in-Pad … POFV": exposed holes let "solder be wicked into the hole, leaving too little on the surface"). Slots can in principle be placed under terminals but this disturbs the current-flow symmetry the Kelvin layout depends on. **Recommendation: if slots are used, keep them in the force path outside the sense window, not under the shunt body.**

### 4. Filled and capped vias (via-in-pad) at high current

**Process reality.** IPC-4761 (2005) Type VII = "filled and capped": filled (Type V) then a metalized cap (IPC-4761 TOC; Macaos, "Via types"; Eurocircuits, "Via Filling"). In the standard process the fill is **non-conductive epoxy resin** — Eurocircuits and Multi-CB both use resin fill, which "will always result in a Type VII" but is non-conductive. Therefore **the fill carries no current**; capacity comes only from the copper barrel plus the thin copper cap. semiconductor.tools confirms "most engineers don't rely on the fill for current carrying." Copper-filled (electroplated) vias are an HDI/microvia process available at premium fabs, not the budget-fab default.

**Cost.** Type VII adds **2.5–3× the cost of unfilled vias** and 2–3 days lead (PCBSync, "IPC-4761 Explained"). At JLCPCB, POFV (resin-filled, copper-capped) is **free on 6–20 layer boards, charged on 4-layer ("POFV for 4-layer PCBs still requires charges"), and not offered on 2-layer** (JLCPCB, "Free Via-in-Pad on 6-20 Layer PCBs with POFV"). JLCPCB's POFV cap plating is quoted as "up to 25 µm" (a maximum/marketing figure, not a guaranteed minimum).

**Advisability under the shunt.** For a Kelvin-sensed 0.5 mΩ element, via-in-pad directly under the terminals is **discouraged**: PCBSync's Kelvin-layout guide (which cites Analog Devices' study as its measured basis) warns that "extremely large pads can cause solder wicking issues during assembly" and that vias in the sense portion of the pad disturb current-flow symmetry; Analog Devices' footprint study found asymmetric pad solder distribution pulls the part during reflow and corrupts the reading. Filled vias also carry solder-void risk that degrades both joint and thermal path (JLCPCB, "Via-in-Pad: Advantages and Challenges"). **Use via-in-pad only in the force path if at all, never between the sense taps.**

### 5. The top-layer-only alternative

**Trace width.** Using the IPC-2221 area formula (the explicit form applied to IPC-2152-class designs; smps.us; PCBSync), for **55 A, 30 °C rise, external (k = 0.048)**: ΔT^0.44 = 30^0.44 ≈ 4.47; required cross-section A = [I/(k·ΔT^0.44)]^(1/0.725) = (55/0.2145)^1.379 ≈ **2,100 mil²**. At 2 oz (2.74 mil) that is **≈ 766 mil ≈ 19.5 mm wide**; at 1 oz (1.37 mil), **≈ 39 mm**. (Assumption set: still air, no extra plane-cooling credit, external layer, FR-4. IPC-2152 with a copper-plane heat-spreading credit typically permits a narrower conductor than IPC-2221; this is the conservative bound. The figure is large but is a pour/polygon, not a discrete trace.)

**Practice.** For currents above ~3 A, published guidance is to use **polygon pours rather than discrete fat traces** and to spread heat with planes (Schemalyzer, "PCB Trace Width Guide": "for currents above 3A, consider polygon pours instead of traces"; the same source's 24 V/10 A worked example uses copper pours + thermal vias). A 2 oz top-layer pour through the shunt keeps the entire high-current path on one layer and eliminates the transition.

**2 oz cost/availability.** 2 oz outer copper is a **standard selectable option** at JLCPCB (1 oz/2 oz standard; JLCPCB, "Copper Weight (Thickness) Guide"); 2-layer boards support up to 4.5 oz, multilayer outer is capped at 2 oz, inner up to 2 oz. 2 oz requires wider min trace/space (0.16 mm vs 0.10 mm for 1 oz). The **price adder is not published** ("always refer to the actual online order page"); third-party estimates put 2 oz at +10–20% (Schemalyzer) — tier-4, confirm at quote tool. Trade-off: 2 oz halves IR loss and roughly doubles current capacity for a given width, and the corpus's heat budget here is set by the shunt, not the pour.

#### (b) Recommended default + reasoning chain
**Default for CEC EPS/PCIe shunt sites: route the high-current path on the TOP (outer) layer as a 2 oz copper pour through the shunt, and avoid the vertical transition at the shunt entirely.** Reasoning: (1) the shunt is the dominant and intentional resistance, so any transition's only "value" is in not corrupting the Kelvin measurement; (2) keeping the path on-layer guarantees the transition is nowhere near the sense window; (3) 2 oz is a standard, low-cost JLCPCB option; (4) it eliminates the via-field reliability concern (barrel cracking under Z-axis CTE cycling is the classic PTH failure mode — SYS Technology CTE guide) and the accuracy concern at once. **This favors the OQ-12 top-layer-rails alternative over the L3-rails-with-via-detour stackup at the shunt sites specifically.**

**Conditions that change this:** if board-area/placement constraints make a ~20 mm-equivalent pour infeasible at a given site, fall back to a **paralleled via field of ≥30 symmetric 0.3–0.5 mm vias placed in the force path outside the sense taps** — the cheapest transition with no exotic process. Escalate to plated slots only if via count crowds the layout; escalate to copper coin essentially never for this current/voltage class.

#### (c) Kelvin placement rule (corpus-entry-ready)
**"Any vertical transition (via field, slot, or via-in-pad) that carries the full shunt current MUST lie entirely outside the four-wire Kelvin sense window — i.e., in the force/current path beyond the two sense taps — never between the sense terminals or under the resistive element. Sense traces exit the pad perpendicular to current flow and drop to an inner layer at the pad edge via small signal vias only."** Source basis: Bourns App Note N1702; Analog Devices / Analog Dialogue Vol. 46 No. 2 (O'Sullivan), "Optimize High-Current Sensing Accuracy…" (22% error from in-loop solder resistance ≈0.144 mΩ; <1% with edge sensing); PCBSync Kelvin-layout guide; Bourns CSS2H-2512 recommended pad layout (separate sense-terminal pads).

## Recommendations
1. **Adopt the top-layer 2 oz pour through the shunt as the OQ-10 default**, and prefer the OQ-12 top-layer-rails stackup at shunt sites. Threshold to revisit: a site that cannot accommodate a ~20 mm-equivalent pour width at 2 oz / 30 °C rise.
2. **Where a transition is forced, use ≥30 symmetric 0.3–0.5 mm vias in the force path outside the sense taps.** Threshold: target ≤1.5 A per via after a 50% crowding margin; verify with a prototype IR-temperature check (keep via-field rise <20 °C, per schemalyzer guidance).
3. **Encode the Kelvin placement rule (6c) verbatim into the corpus** as a hard layout constraint.
4. **Reject copper coin and via-in-pad-under-shunt** for this current/voltage class; reserve plated slots only as a space-saving fallback, kept out of the sense window.
5. **Run the (6d) checklist at the JLCPCB quote tool before committing** — especially the guaranteed barrel plating, which directly sets via count; if the fab will only guarantee ~18 µm, recompute per-via capacity downward (~0.8–1.0 A at 10 °C) and increase the via count accordingly.

## Caveats
- JLCPCB's public capabilities page is JavaScript-rendered; the exact barrel-plating µm, slot, and pricing values are not in public static documentation and require quote-tool confirmation. The ~18 µm barrel figure is from an unofficial support reply, not a spec sheet, and "up to 25 µm" POFV plating is a maximum/marketing figure, not a guaranteed minimum.
- IPC-2152/2221 and the standards themselves are paywalled; the numeric via/trace figures here are drawn from fabricator and tool implementations of those standards (Tier 2) and should be re-derived against the purchased standard for any safety-critical claim.
- The 20–30% via thermal-coupling derate, the "50 A+ slot," and the copper-coin "2× via array" claims are vendor/tier-4 corroboration, not load-bearing; treat as indicative.
- The trace-width result is the conservative IPC-2221 bound; an IPC-2152 calculation with a plane-cooling credit may permit a narrower pour. Per-via resistance (~0.8 mΩ) varies with the wall-area assumption (2.2–3.3 × 10⁻⁸ m²) and board thickness; the ~37 µΩ field figure (≈7% of the shunt) is robust to that range.
- Connector wattage figures (EPS/PCIe) trace to the Molex Mini-Fit Jr. contact datasheet (8 A/3-circuit → 288 W) and the Intel/ATX design guide; the lower "150 W" PCIe figure is a card power-budget convention, not a contact limit.
