# DC Series Arc-Fault Signatures: Grounding the CEC Max Arc-Detection Architecture (OQ-16 / OQ-17 / OQ-18)

## TL;DR
- The spec's "2–5 MHz arc band" is **not supported** by the peer-reviewed and national-lab literature and is directly contradicted by measurement: validated DC series-arc detectors cluster in the **~100 Hz to ~100 kHz** band, and Sandia explicitly found "little or no difference in the arcing noise content when compared to the baseline" from 100 kHz to 5 MHz. CEC should re-center its analysis band to roughly **10–100 kHz**, not 2–5 MHz.
- At 12 V a *sustained* series arc is physically marginal-to-impossible: minimum arc voltage for relevant contact metals is ~12–16 V (copper ~13 V, tin ~13.5 V, gold 15 V from contact theory), so a 12 V rail sits at/below the boundary. The detectable phenomenon is therefore **intermittent micro-arc/showering-arc bursts and rising contact resistance**, not a stable free-burning arc — which argues for reframing the Max product around contact-degradation trending (already covered by dV/dI) with HF burst detection as a complement.
- A 1 mΩ 2512 shunt with <2 nH is **conditional / no** as an HF pickup: above its inductive corner (~80 kHz) its impedance is inductance-dominated (12.6 mΩ at 1 MHz, 62.8 mΩ at 5 MHz), so it acts as a crude di/dt sensor that *helps* HF pickup but *destroys* amplitude calibration. It is not a validated HF arc pickup; peer-reviewed HF arc detectors use CTs / Rogowski coils.

## Key Findings
1. **Arc current noise is broadband 1/f ("pink") noise** superimposed on the DC current — repeatedly validated (Sandia; Northeastern dissertation; IEEE conference arc-modeling work). Power spectral density falls roughly as 1/√f.
2. **The discriminating band is low, ~100 Hz–100 kHz.** Sandia (Johnson et al., SAND2011-3871C / 37th IEEE PVSC, 2011) measured clear arc-vs-baseline separation only in 100 Hz–100 kHz, with no usable separation from 100 kHz to 5 MHz. A peer-reviewed PV review (IJSGCE) recommends 1–100 kHz and warns RF/antenna effects corrupt content above 100 kHz.
3. **The 12 V boundary is the central physics issue.** Minimum arc voltage for copper/tin/gold is ~12–16 V (Holm/Slade contact theory); automotive literature is explicit that 14 V cannot sustain a stable arc whereas 42 V readily can. A 12 V GPU rail cannot reliably sustain a stable series arc; the realistic signatures are micro-arc/showering bursts and fretting-corrosion resistance noise.
4. **Validated capture chains are modest:** PV arc studies sample 10 kS/s–10 MS/s; dominant peer-reviewed designs use 50 kS/s–1 MS/s with 12–24-bit ADCs. UL/EDN reference guidance cites 16-bit @ 250 kS/s over 40–100 kHz.
5. **The HF arc sensor of choice is a CT or Rogowski coil, not a shunt.** Shunts are validated only for low-frequency/transient (rate-of-change) DC arc detection (Naidu & Schoepf, 2006).
6. **Trip-time budgets:** IEC 63027:2023 requires detection/interruption within 2.5 s or before arc energy exceeds 750 J; UL 1699B uses graded clearing times (2 s at 300 W down to 0.8 s at 900 W). Field confirmation windows are 50–150 ms.

## Details

### 1. Physics and spectral signature of DC series arcs (peer-reviewed foundation)

The foundational, repeatedly validated result is that a DC series arc injects **broadband 1/f ("pink") noise** onto the DC current. Sandia National Laboratories (Johnson, Pahl, Luebke, et al., "Photovoltaic DC Arc Fault Detector Testing at Sandia National Laboratories," SAND2011-3871C, 37th IEEE PVSC, 2011) states the arc "generates 1/f ('pink') AC noise on top of the DC current." This is corroborated by a modeling paper (IEEE, "DC Arc Fault Model Superimposing Multiple Random Arc Noise States," 2019), which derives a "current noise power spectral density inversely proportional to the square root of the frequency," and by the Northeastern University doctoral dissertation on PV DC arc-fault detection. Several PV studies also note the arc noise rises with arc current (higher DC current → more arc noise) and is largely insensitive to source voltage in the 200–400 V range (MDPI *Fire*, 2026).

**The arc-sustainability boundary at 12 V is decisive and under-appreciated in the spec.** Holm's classic contact theory and subsequent work establish a minimum arc voltage (Vmin) and minimum arc current (Imin) below which an arc cannot be sustained. For relevant metals: copper ~13 V, tin ~13.5 V, gold 15 V at 0.38 A; a contact patent states flatly that "metals all have arc voltages of between 12 Volts and 16 Volts" (US 8,697,247), and another gives "the minimum arc discharge current Im is 0.38 A, and the minimum arc discharge voltage Vm is 15 V" for gold (US 7,486,488). Critically:

- An inventor-grounded patent states DC arcs "generally do not persist unless the voltage across the air gap is at least 14 volts. Thus, arcs in traditional 12-volt automotive electrical systems have traditionally been relatively uncommon and/or less severe" (US 6,683,766).
- The 42 V PowerNet arc-fault research program exists *precisely because* 14 V marginally sustains arcs while 42 V readily does. Schoepf & Naidu (49th IEEE Holm Conf., 2003) characterize the 14 VDC network as one where "no serious consequences are associated with plugging and unplugging under load due to very short break arcs (the system voltage is approximately the same as the minimum arc voltage of the contact material)." A connector patent reinforces: "Arcing has not been a significant problem for standard 14 volt electrical systems, because 14 volts is below the minimum arc voltage for most contact materials. A stable arc typically cannot exist below 15 volts… All contact metals can sustain a stable arc above 20 volts" (US 6,666,698).
- Below Vmin/Imin, the literature documents **micro-arcing** (Ben Jemaa, Hasegawa, IEEE Holm Conf.: arcs of 5–100 ns persisting below classical Im/Vm down to a material-dependent limiting current "generally below 100 mA"), **showering-arc** phenomena at contact bounce/separation, and **glow discharge**.

**Boundary verdict:** At 12 V / 9 A per pin, a stable free-burning series arc is physically marginal-to-impossible for copper/tin contacts. The detectable electrical phenomenon at a degrading 12VHPWR contact is far more likely to be (a) intermittent micro-arc/showering bursts during contact micro-motion (fretting, vibration, thermal cycling), and (b) rising and fluctuating contact resistance from fretting corrosion. Fretting-corrosion literature (Antler reviews; ScienceDirect studies) confirms the early signature is "intermittent high resistance events … before significant changes in average resistance," with magnitudes from tens of mΩ to ohms, plus "electrical distortions (or noise)."

### 2. The frequency-band question — grounding / correcting "2–5 MHz"

This is the clearest correction in the dive. **Validated DC arc detectors cluster low, not in the MHz range:**

- **Sandia (SAND2011-3871C, OSTI full text):** "From 1-100 Hz and 100 kHz to 5 MHz there is little or no difference in the arcing noise content when compared to the baseline … However, for the frequencies between 100 Hz-100 kHz, there is a clear separation between the arcing noise and the baseline for all strings." Inverter switching harmonics span "1 kHz to >100 kHz." Data were captured at a **10 MHz sample rate on a 500 MHz scope** — so the absence of MHz-band separation is a measured result, not an instrumentation limit.
- **Peer-reviewed PV review (IJSGCE, "PV arc-fault feature extraction and detection"):** "favorable frequency bandwidths between 1-100 kHz are recommended for DC arc fault detection," because below ~100–1000 Hz you hit irradiance/120 Hz/60 Hz noise, and above 100 kHz "RF noise can be transmitted and inducted by PV cells, modules, DC cables… high frequency will be attenuated by weak or unshielded connections."
- **MDPI Applied Sciences (AR-model detector):** arc HF content "typically concentrated in the 10−80 kHz frequency band."
- **Multiple peer-reviewed detectors** use sub-bands within this: a 23–35 kHz avoidance band (arc-current-entropy paper, which found inverter noise dominates there), 5–40 kHz, 10–70 kHz (Gu et al.), a 2.4–39 kHz band-pass, and 0–100 kHz integration (capacitor-current localization, *Solar Energy*).
- **MHz-range counterpoints are weaker and/or non-PV:** an AIAA 2003 paper monitored ~19 MHz on radial DC distribution; a Rogowski-coil DC paper claims a 1 kHz–1 MHz band; an AC arc paper claims content "2 kHz to 20 MHz." These are real but application-specific (long aerial lines, AC mains) or lower-SNR than the kHz band.

**Why low wins (physics):** Arc noise PSD falls as 1/√f, so the absolute arc signal is largest at low frequency. SMPS/inverter harmonics also concentrate at low frequency, but the *contrast* (arc-to-background) is empirically best in 10–100 kHz — above the switching fundamentals and their strongest low harmonics yet below the RF/antenna regime where wiring parasitics dominate and the arc signal has already decayed.

**Band verdict for CEC:** "2–5 MHz" is **unsupported** by peer-reviewed/lab evidence and is contradicted by Sandia's direct measurement. The literature supports **~10 kHz to ~100 kHz** as the primary analysis band, with a possible low extension toward ~1 kHz. The PC environment differs from PV (GPU VRM switching fundamentals of hundreds of kHz with harmonics into the MHz range), which is a strong argument to **characterize CEC's own background spectrum before locking the band** — but it does **not** support jumping to 2–5 MHz, where arc PSD is lowest and VRM harmonics are still present.

### 3. Discrimination and false positives (peer-reviewed treatment)

Separating arc bursts from VRM/SMPS switching and load transients is well studied:

- **Switching noise is structured; arc noise is not.** Inverter/SMPS noise is harmonic and relatively stationary; arc noise is non-stationary broadband 1/f. Peer-reviewed detectors exploit this with spectral entropy (arc current entropy differs from inverter noise), autoregressive modeling (arc = nonstationary random vs. PWM noise = not), wavelet/time-frequency methods, kurtosis/statistical features, and ML classifiers (CNN/BiLSTM, S-transform + PSD).
- **Crosstalk is a documented false-positive source.** Sandia found arcing frequency content and crosstalk noise on parallel strings "very similar," concluding "more than just the frequency content … may be required to differentiate." This is directly relevant to CEC's per-pin question (§4).
- **UL 1699B explicitly tests unwanted tripping** against inverter noise, EMI, fast transients, induced RF, ESD, voltage dips/surges. The Sandia "Arc-Fault Unwanted Tripping Survey with UL 1699B-Listed Products" (Johnson et al., IEEE PVSC, 2015) found that beyond UL 1699B's baseline, many listed products "were sensitive to unwanted tripping or were ineffective at detecting harmful arc-fault events" — a caution that lab certification ≠ field robustness.
- **Persistence logic is the workhorse false-positive defense.** Detectors require the signature to persist (an arc, once struck, persists), discarding short spurious bursts; patents describe accumulator/"bucket" schemes tuned to meet clearing time with margin while rejecting transients. **This is in direct tension with the 12 V finding:** if the real 12 V signature is intermittent bursts rather than a persisting arc, classic persistence logic will reject it — a key architecture implication.

**Time windows / trip budgets (from standards):** IEC 63027:2023 verbatim: "The AFPE shall detect the arc and interrupt it within 2,5 s or before the arc energy exceeds 750 J, whichever occurs first. If the AFPE can extinguish the arc before exceeding 200 J and within 2,5 s, then annunciation of an arc detection is not required" (auto-reconnect 4× per 24 h, then manual). UL 1699B's arcing-test table specifies graded clearing times from 2 s (300 W) down to 0.8 s (900 W). Industry/EDN summaries cite "16-bit ADC @ 250 kS/s, 40–100 kHz band" as a compliant reference design. Confirmation windows in fielded designs are 50–150 ms (Sandia noted some products tripped as fast as 62 ms). Treat blog claims of "0.2 s / 0.5 s" as non-authoritative.

### 4. Sensor and capture-chain requirements (OQ-17 and OQ-18)

**Sample rate / bit depth (OQ-17):** Peer-reviewed and reference DC-arc chains are modest:
- Sandia characterization rig: 10 MS/s (for research characterization, not the deployed detector).
- Peer-reviewed PV chain (*Measurement*, 2021, low-frequency spectral analysis): NI-9239, **24-bit delta-sigma, 10 kS/s used** (50 kS/s max), alias-free to 0.453·fs; current sensed with a Fluke A40B shunt (accurate to 100 kHz) and a LEM PR 30 Hall clamp (DC–100 kHz).
- DCAMamba arc dataset (arXiv): **4 MS/s, 12-bit.**
- Reference SAR-ADC arc SoC: **1 MS/s.**
- EDN/UL reference: **16-bit @ 250 kS/s, 40–100 kHz.**

To resolve up to ~100 kHz you need ≥200 kS/s (Nyquist) and realistically **500 kS/s–1 MS/s** for clean spectral features and margin. **There is no peer-reviewed basis for the multi-MS/s rates a 2–5 MHz band would demand.** If CEC re-centers to ≤100 kHz, a **~500 kS/s–1 MS/s, 14–16-bit** chain is amply supported, and a *single shared fast channel (multiplexed) or one modest ADC per pin* is sufficient — six concurrent multi-MS/s channels are not justified by any validated arc study.

**Dynamic range:** The literature reports arc noise in *relative/spectral* terms, not absolute mA — a genuine gap (flagged). The one concrete absolute anchor (a patent, US 9,945,894) shows a band-limited (1.5–45 kHz) noise floor of **<3 mA on a 30 A circuit**, with the arc stepping clearly above it — a signal-to-DC ratio of ~10⁻³–10⁻⁴. To resolve a few-mA AC feature on a 9 A per-pin DC level you need roughly **12–14 effective bits *after* removing the DC pedestal**, which strongly favors **AC-coupling / DC-blocking the fast channel** (or a high-pass front end) so the ADC span serves the small AC signal rather than the large DC pedestal. This is exactly why CT/Rogowski pickups (which inherently reject DC) are popular.

**OQ-18 — the shunt verdict, with math:** For a 1 mΩ 2512 shunt the DC signal is 1 mV/A (9 mV at 9 A — small but workable with a good instrumentation amplifier). Parasitic inductance dominates at HF. Impedance Z = √(R² + (2πfL)²):
- With **L = 2 nH:** X_L = 2πfL = **12.6 mΩ at 1 MHz**, **62.8 mΩ at 5 MHz.**
- The corner where X_L = R (1 mΩ) is f = R/(2πL) = **~80 kHz.** Above ~80 kHz the response is inductance-dominated.
- Consequence: at 1 MHz the shunt impedance is ~12.6× its DC value and rising +20 dB/decade — the element has *become a di/dt sensor*. This **helps** HF pickup (the rising response boosts the small HF arc signal) but **destroys amplitude calibration** (you cannot infer current magnitude in that band without an RC pole-zero compensation network matched to the shunt's L/R).

For context, manufacturer data place a 2512 metal-strip shunt at 2–5 nH (Bourns CSS2H-2512: "<2 nH" or "<5 nH"; CSI2H-2512: "<2 nH"; Analog Devices: ~5 nH typical, with a worked example showing a 5 mΩ/5 nH shunt usable only to ~160 kHz). An OMICRON-Lab measurement of a 4-wire current-sense resistor showed 5.22 nH at 1 MHz, confirming the range.

**OQ-18 conclusion: conditional / no.** A 1 mΩ 2512 shunt is *not* a validated, calibrated HF arc pickup. No peer-reviewed paper validates a metal-strip shunt as a multi-MHz arc sensor; the canonical shunt-based DC arc detector — Naidu, Schoepf & Gopalakrishnan, "Arc Fault Detection Scheme for 42-V Automotive DC Networks Using Current Shunt," *IEEE Trans. Power Electron.*, 21(3):633–639, 2006 (DOI 10.1109/TPEL.2006.872385) — is a **rate-of-change/step detector operating at DC–low-kHz**, using a comparator-plus-low-pass-filter threshold (amplifier gain 1.1) and reporting **no** numeric di/dt threshold or HF cutoff. It should **not** be cited as MHz-shunt precedent. If CEC re-centers to ≤100 kHz, the shunt is below its ~80 kHz inductive corner over most of that band and is usable for amplitude (with mild HF correction near 80–100 kHz). For any HF burst path, the validated approach is a **dedicated di/dt element (Rogowski / PCB air-coil) or HF CT per pin**, or to knowingly use the shunt's inductive term as an uncalibrated di/dt sensor.

**Per-pin vs aggregate (OQ-17/18):** Physics: a series arc in one pin modulates that pin's current; because the six power pins are paralleled, the perturbation partially redistributes to the other pins. Sandia's crosstalk finding (arcing and crosstalk spectra "very similar") shows an aggregate/common-path sensor *will* see the broadband signature but **cannot localize** it, and a per-pin sensor can be fooled by crosstalk from an adjacent path. For CEC, an **aggregate common-path HF sensor is sufficient to *detect* an arc/burst event**; **per-pin sensing is needed only for localization** and for the contact-degradation (per-pin dV/dI resistance) trending that is the more defensible primary signal at 12 V. Multi-string AFD patents confirm per-channel current sensors are used specifically to handle adjacent-conductor crosstalk.

### 5. The 12 V verdict and architecture implication

Synthesizing §1–4: at **12 V / 9 A per pin, validated physics says the detectable phenomenon is predominantly contact degradation (rising/fluctuating resistance from fretting corrosion) with intermittent micro-arc/showering-arc bursts at contact micro-motion — not a sustained series arc.** A stable free-burning arc requires ≥~12–16 V across the gap; a 12 V rail with a sub-volt-to-few-volt contact drop cannot maintain the gap voltage needed once the contact separates enough to draw a real arc — the supply collapses the arc. The exception: a momentarily high-resistance contact can develop several volts across it and micro-arc transiently, but this is bursty, not sustained.

**Implied architecture:**
- **Primary:** slow thermal/resistance trending (per-pin dV/dI). This is the physically dominant, most reliable early-warning signal, and CEC already has it. It should be the headline detection mechanism.
- **Secondary/complementary:** transient **burst** detection (not continuous band-power monitoring of a persistent arc) in the ~10–100 kHz band, tuned to catch short micro-arc events. Persistence/accumulator logic from PV AFCIs must be **re-tuned** — a 12 V micro-arc will not persist like a 300 W PV arc, so a burst-rate metric is more appropriate than a "must persist for N ms" gate.
- **Not recommended:** marketing "arc detection" as a primary, standalone, sustained-arc capability at 12 V. The literature does not support that a classic sustained series arc is the dominant hazard at 12 V; the dominant hazard is thermal runaway from high-resistance contacts — consistent with the field pattern of melted 12VHPWR connectors, which is a thermal/resistance failure rather than a sustained-arc fire.

**Product-shaping recommendation:** Reframe the Max-tier claim from "series arc detection" toward **"connector-degradation and micro-arc event detection"** — contact-resistance trending as primary, HF burst detection as corroborating evidence. This is both more honest to the physics and more defensible in marketing/liability terms.

### 6. Deliverable summary

**(a) 2–5 MHz band verdict: UNSUPPORTED.** Literature supports ~10–100 kHz (extendable down to ~1 kHz). Sandia measured no arc/baseline separation 100 kHz–5 MHz; PV reviews recommend 1–100 kHz and warn of RF corruption above 100 kHz. Action: re-center to ~10–100 kHz after characterizing CEC's own VRM background spectrum.

**(b) Recommended capture-chain parameters (with source basis):**
- Analog bandwidth: **DC–~150 kHz** for the fast/AC channel (covers the validated 10–100 kHz band with margin). Basis: Sandia 100 Hz–100 kHz separation; IJSGCE 1–100 kHz.
- Sample rate: **500 kS/s–1 MS/s** per active channel. Basis: EDN/UL 250 kS/s reference; SAR-ADC 1 MS/s SoC; NI-9239 chains at 10–50 kS/s for sub-25 kHz work.
- Bit depth / dynamic range: **14–16 bit with AC-coupling / DC-blocking** so the small (mA-scale) AC arc feature uses the ADC span rather than the 9 A DC pedestal. Basis: patent <3 mA-on-30 A absolute figure (signal/DC ~10⁻³–10⁻⁴); EDN 16-bit recommendation. (Flag: absolute amplitude is a literature gap.)
- Integration/confirmation window: **50–150 ms burst confirmation**, well inside the IEC 63027 2.5 s / UL 1699B 0.8–2 s budgets.

**(c) OQ-18 shunt verdict: CONDITIONAL / NO for HF.** 1 mΩ 2512 (<2 nH): inductive corner f = R/2πL ≈ 80 kHz; Z = 12.6 mΩ @ 1 MHz, 62.8 mΩ @ 5 MHz. Usable (with mild correction) for amplitude up to ~80–100 kHz; above that it is an uncalibrated di/dt sensor. Not a validated HF arc pickup. If re-centered to ≤100 kHz, the shunt is adequate as the primary current/degradation sensor; for any HF burst path, add a dedicated di/dt element or HF CT.

**(d) Per-pin vs aggregate HF sensing: aggregate suffices for detection; per-pin for localization + degradation trending.** Recommend an aggregate common-path HF burst sensor + per-pin shunt resistance trending (which CEC already has). Six concurrent multi-MS/s channels are not justified by any validated study.

**(e) 12 V sustainability verdict + framing: sustained arc marginal/unlikely; reframe toward contact-degradation + micro-arc burst detection.** Contact-resistance/dV-dI trending as primary; HF burst detection as complement.

## Recommendations
1. **Re-center the analysis band to ~10–100 kHz and remove "2–5 MHz" from the spec.** First action: capture CEC's own VRM/SMPS background spectrum at idle and under high-di/dt load transients (the documented 2–3× excursions over 100 µs–10 ms), then pick the sub-band with best arc-burst-to-background contrast. Threshold to revisit: if measured background in 10–100 kHz is dominated by VRM harmonics, use a notch or a narrower clean sub-band rather than moving up to MHz (where arc PSD is lowest and VRM harmonics persist).
2. **Make per-pin contact-resistance (dV/dI) trending the primary Max-tier detection mechanism;** position HF burst detection as a complement, not the headline. Reframe marketing from "arc detection" to "connector-degradation / micro-arc event detection." Benchmark that would change this: bench evidence of repeatable, sustained (>100 ms) broadband 1/f arc signatures at 12 V would justify elevating arc detection to primary.
3. **Spec the fast chain at 500 kS/s–1 MS/s, 14–16-bit, AC-coupled.** Do not build six concurrent multi-MS/s channels; an aggregate common-path HF burst sensor plus per-pin shunt resistance trending meets the validated requirements. Add per-pin HF only if localization becomes a product requirement.
4. **Do not rely on the 1 mΩ shunt as a calibrated HF pickup.** Use it for current/resistance trending (its strength). If HF bursts are pursued, add a dedicated di/dt element (PCB Rogowski / air-coil) or HF CT, or knowingly use the shunt's inductive regime as an uncalibrated di/dt sensor with the L/R-matched RC compensation network.
5. **Re-tune persistence logic for bursts, not sustained arcs.** Use a burst-rate/accumulator metric appropriate to intermittent 12 V micro-arcing; validate against IEC 63027/UL 1699B timing (50–150 ms confirmation, <2.5 s action).
6. **Run a bench characterization** on deliberately degraded 12VHPWR contacts (fretted, partially mated, thermally aged) to obtain the missing absolute arc-noise amplitude and the real spectral signature in CEC's environment before signing corpus numeric claims. This single experiment resolves the two biggest evidence gaps (absolute amplitude and the PC-specific band) at once.

## Caveats
- No peer-reviewed arc-fault study addresses 12VHPWR / 12V-2x6 connectors or the specific PC noise environment; all conclusions are transferred from PV (≥80 V) and automotive (12/42 V) literature and require bench validation.
- Absolute arc-noise current amplitude lacks a peer-reviewed source; the dynamic-range recommendation is provisional.
- The 12 V sustainability conclusion is well-grounded in contact physics, but it means CEC's detectable target is bursty/intermittent — harder to detect reliably than a sustained PV arc, and conventional AFCI persistence logic may suppress it.
- UL 1699B / IEC 63027 are designed for ≥80 V PV systems; their thresholds (300 W minimum arc, 200–750 J) do not map directly to a 12 V / 600 W connector and should be treated as methodological templates, not pass/fail targets for CEC.
- "MHz-range" arc claims in the literature are not fabricated — they exist — but they are predominantly AC, aerial-line, or single-source results with lower SNR than the kHz band; they do not validate a 2–5 MHz band for a DC series arc on a short, shielded PC power harness.
